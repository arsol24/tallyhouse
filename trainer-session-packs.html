How to keep track of prepaid training sessions

Selling packs of 5, 10 or 20 sessions is great for cash flow. It only works if you always know who has sessions left.

A client pays for ten sessions up front. Six weeks later, neither of you is sure how many are left. Sound familiar? A few simple habits fix it.

1. Record each pack once, when you sell it

Write down the client, the date, the pack size, the price paid and any expiry date. This single line is the pack. Don't re-enter the money every time they train.

2. Log every session against the pack

After each session, note the date and which pack it came from. Give these sessions a price of zero, because the client already paid. That way the money is counted one time only, on the day of the sale.

3. Work out sessions left

Sessions left = pack size minus sessions logged. Check it at each session and tell the client the number, for example "that's 7 of 10 used".

4. Set a "running low" point

Pick a number, such as two sessions left, and use it as your cue to offer a new pack. Also watch for expiry dates, so a pack doesn't quietly lapse.

5. Know what you still owe

Unused sessions are work you have been paid for but not yet done. Add them up now and then, so a good month of sales doesn't look like free money.

Not tax advice. How prepaid income is treated for tax depends on where you live. Check local rules or ask an accountant.
